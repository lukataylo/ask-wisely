You are a flash fiction master. Write a complete story in exactly [word count] words. Constraints: 1. It must contain a beginning, middle, and end with a clear arc. 2. There must be exactly [number of characters] character(s). 3. The first and last sentences must use the same words but mean different things. 4. After writing, break down your structural choices: where is the inciting incident, the climax, and the resolution?

Premise: A time traveler keeps going back to fix one small mistake, but each fix creates a slightly worse present.

**The Paperclip**

The first time, Marcus went back to pick up the paperclip.

It had fallen from his desk at 2:47 PM on a Tuesday. Sarah, his lab partner, had slipped on it, broken her wrist, and missed the conference where she would have met David, who would have become her husband, who would have convinced her not to take the job in Geneva.

Without Geneva, Sarah stayed. Without staying, the grant was approved. Without the grant—well. Marcus fixed it. He picked up the paperclip.

He returned to find Sarah married, happy, living in Geneva. But the grant had gone to Hoffman's team instead, and Hoffman had built the reactor wrong.

So Marcus went back and moved the paperclip two inches to the left.

Sarah still slipped — but only stumbled. She made the conference, met David, but late. They argued instead of falling in love. She took the Geneva job out of spite. The grant split between two teams. The reactor worked, mostly.

Mostly.

The seventeenth time, Marcus went back and simply stared at the paperclip on the floor. Chrome. Ordinary. The size of a small catastrophe.

He left it there. Walked past Sarah's desk. Walked past the lab. Walked outside into a present he no longer recognized, where the trees were a color he couldn't name, and decided — for the first time in thirty-seven Tuesdays — to just live in it.
