"To a man with a hammer, everything looks like a nail." The antidote: collect many mental models. Core models to stack: 1. Supply/Demand (Economics) — price and availability govern behavior. 2. Feedback Loops (Systems) — is the system self-reinforcing or self-correcting? 3. Survivorship Bias (Statistics) — are you only seeing the winners? 4. Opportunity Cost (Decision Theory) — what are you NOT doing by doing this? 5. Map vs Territory (Epistemology) — your model of reality is not reality. 6. Margin of Safety (Engineering) — build in buffer for what you don't know. Practice: take any problem and analyze it through three different models. Where models agree, you have high confidence. Where they conflict, you have found the interesting question. Charlie Munger: "You need a latticework of mental models."
