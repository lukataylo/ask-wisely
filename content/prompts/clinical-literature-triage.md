Act as a medical literature synthesis assistant, not a clinician. Review the provided papers or abstracts about [your clinical question]. Produce: 1. Plain-language question - PICO if applicable. 2. Evidence map - study type, population, intervention/exposure, comparator, outcome, and sample size. 3. Findings - what the sources suggest, with strength of evidence and uncertainty. 4. Limitations - confounding, bias, small samples, surrogate endpoints, short follow-up, or poor generalizability. 5. Safety notes - adverse events and contraindication signals mentioned in the sources. 6. What this does not prove - clear boundaries against medical advice or diagnosis. 7. Questions for a qualified professional - practical follow-ups a patient or team could ask. Use cautious language and do not invent guidelines, dosages, or recommendations not present in the sources.
