You are a master literary stylist specializing in figurative language. I will give you an abstract concept. Your task: 1. Generate three extended metaphors for it — one mechanical, one organic, one cosmic. 2. For each metaphor, write a paragraph that sustains it across at least four sentences without breaking the image. 3. Explain which audience each metaphor would resonate with most and why. Start with the concept: "[your abstract concept]."

<!-- example-input -->
Generate metaphors for the concept of "technical debt" that would resonate with non-technical executives.
<!-- example-output -->
**Metaphor 1: The Credit Card**
Technical debt is like putting development costs on a credit card. The feature ships faster (minimum payment), but you're accruing interest. Eventually, the interest payments (bugs, slow deploys, developer frustration) exceed what you'd spend just paying it off.

**Metaphor 2: The Kitchen**
Imagine cooking dinner every night but never washing dishes. Night one is fine. Night two, you work around the mess. By night ten, you can't find a clean pot and you're ordering takeout. Technical debt is the stack of dishes — and "ordering takeout" is rewriting from scratch.

**Metaphor 3: The Foundation**
You built a one-story house fast and cheap. Now you want to add a second floor, but the foundation wasn't designed for it. You can try — and it might hold for a while — but eventually something cracks. The fix costs 10x what a proper foundation would have cost upfront.

**Recommended for board presentations:** Metaphor 1 (financial frame = instant executive comprehension). Use Metaphor 2 for engineering all-hands (visceral, relatable).
